# Persuasion — the primitives

These are cross-cutting levers, not standalone tactics — layer them into offers ([offers.md](offers.md)), copy ([copy.md](copy.md)), and distribution ([distribution.md](distribution.md)). They work because they match how people actually decide: evolved social heuristics running beneath conscious reasoning. Misused, they read as manipulation and backfire — often permanently.

---

### Cialdini's 7 Principles of Persuasion — Robert Cialdini, Influence (1984) / Pre-Suasion (2016)

*Seven psychological levers — rooted in evolved social heuristics — that reliably move people toward yes when applied before, during, or after a persuasive ask.*

**When:** Any time you need to increase compliance, conversion, or cooperation: product launches, email sequences, landing pages, sales conversations, onboarding flows, fundraising asks, or policy adoption campaigns. Reach for the specific principle whose mechanism matches the friction blocking the yes.

| Principle | Mechanism (why it works) | Apply it (ethical marketing tactic) | Over-use warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Reciprocity** | People feel obligated to return favors. The norm triggers automatically — even for unsolicited gifts — and scales with perceived generosity, unexpectedness, and personalization. Regan (1971): recipients of an unsolicited Coke bought twice as many raffle tickets from the giver. | Give first, give genuinely, give before the ask. The gift must feel free of strings to activate the norm. Personalize it ("for you specifically"). Make the give meaningful enough that not reciprocating would feel uncomfortable. *Template: "Before we talk about [product], here is [specific free resource] that solves [exact problem] — no strings attached."* | Attaching conditions ("free guide — just enter your email") converts the gift into a transaction and kills the obligation. |
| **2. Commitment & Consistency** | Once people commit — especially publicly, in writing, or through effortful action — they align subsequent behavior to protect a consistent self-image. Mechanism: cognitive dissonance. Commitments are strongest when active, public, effortful, and freely chosen. | Extract a small, genuine yes that aligns with your larger ask. Frame the main offer as consistent with that earlier commitment. Sequence: micro-yes → medium-yes → full offer. *Template: "You told us [small prior commitment]. Here is [next step] that's consistent with that."* | Manufactured micro-commitments (dark patterns, forced clicks) backfire — users reject the larger ask harder to protect their autonomy. |
| **3. Social Proof** | Under uncertainty, people infer correct behavior from what similar others are doing. Effect amplifies with (a) number of others and (b) similarity of those others. Cialdini's towel-reuse study (Goldstein, Cialdini & Griskevicius, 2008, *Journal of Consumer Research*): "Most guests in this room reuse towels" outperformed generic environmental appeals by ~26%. | Display proof from the most similar possible cohort to your target — not the most impressive cohort. Specificity beats scale: "4,312 SaaS founders" outperforms "10,000 businesses." Match proof to funnel stage: volume proof for acquisition, similar-case testimonials for decision. | Showing proof from the wrong cohort (enterprise logos to SMB prospects) signals misfit. Generic star ratings have become visual noise. |
| **4. Authority** | People defer to credible experts on complex or high-stakes decisions. Authority cues: titles, credentials, track record, relevant third-party endorsement. Cialdini's key point: authority must be signaled **before** the ask, not assumed. | Demonstrate credentials in the specific domain of the ask before making it. Third-party validation outperforms self-assertion. Match authority type to audience: practitioners trust track records; academics trust peer citations; consumers trust recognizable institutional logos. | Authority in one domain does not transfer to unrelated domains — celebrity endorsements outside their expertise register as inauthentic and reduce trust. |
| **5. Liking** | People comply more readily with people they like. Three drivers: (a) similarity — shared identity, values, or background; (b) genuine compliments — flattery works even when recipients know it, if it contains a grain of truth; (c) cooperative association — working toward a shared goal. *Distinct from Unity: Liking is individual-to-individual affinity; Unity is shared group identity.* | Uncover real similarities between your brand persona and your audience before pitching. Mirror their language; reference their real struggles. Use specific, genuine compliments rather than generic praise. | Fabricated similarity or fake compliments are detectable and destroy trust permanently. The mechanism requires genuine common ground. |
| **6. Scarcity** | People assign higher value to rare or dwindling opportunities. Mechanism: loss aversion (Kahneman & Tversky) — the pain of losing access is felt roughly 2× more intensely than the pleasure of gaining it. Two types: quantity scarcity ("only 3 left") and time scarcity ("offer ends Friday"). | Only communicate scarcity that is real. State the specific constraint *and its reason*: "We cap beta cohorts at 50 so each founder gets a live Q&A slot" is more persuasive than a bare countdown because it explains the why. Frame CTA in loss language: "Claim your spot" over "Sign up." Booking.com's "Only 2 rooms left at this price" works because the scarcity is genuine (dynamic inventory) and specific (this price tier). | Fake scarcity — evergreen countdown timers that reset, permanently "low stock" badges — creates urgency fatigue and, when discovered, destroys brand credibility. Consumers are increasingly scarcity-literate. |
| **7. Unity** *(added Pre-Suasion, 2016)* | People are most influenced by those they perceive as part of the same "we" — same family, tribe, political group, or tight-knit community. Shared identity creates in-group favoritism: we assign positive intent and higher credibility to in-group members. Cialdini notes Unity acts as an accelerator — when present, all other principles work more effectively. *Original six are from Influence (1984); Unity is the seventh, introduced in Pre-Suasion (2016).* | Identify the real group identity your best customers already claim and make it explicit in your positioning. Co-creation rituals strengthen Unity. Use "us/we" language grounded in a label the audience already holds, not an aspirational one you're projecting onto them. Notion's early positioning — "builders and makers who refuse to use broken tools" — activated Unity, not just Liking, producing extreme in-group word-of-mouth. | Claiming a group identity your brand does not authentically belong to reads as pandering. Unity built on performative identity signaling is immediately recognized as manipulation. |

**Watch out:**

- **Reciprocity** — conditions on the gift kill the norm entirely.
- **Commitment** — must be freely chosen; coerced micro-yeses produce harder rejection downstream.
- **Social proof** — wrong cohort signals misfit, not validation.
- **Authority** — domain-matched only; borrowed credibility from mismatched experts reduces trust.
- **Liking** — fabricated similarity is detectable and permanently damaging.
- **Scarcity** — fake scarcity is increasingly detected; when caught, it collapses brand credibility.
- **Unity** — performative identity claims read as manipulation.
- **Over-stacking** — deploying all seven simultaneously signals desperation, not persuasion. Match the principle to the specific friction blocking the yes.

> ⚠️ Specific conversion-uplift percentages and A/B test figures (from CXL, Drip, Booking.com case studies) cited in secondary literature should be treated as illustrative benchmarks, not audited figures. The 26% towel-reuse figure is primary source: Goldstein, Cialdini & Griskevicius (2008), *Journal of Consumer Research*.

### Engineering Consent — Edward Bernays, Crystallizing Public Opinion (1923) / Propaganda (1928) / "The Engineering of Consent" (1947)

*The public doesn't weigh claims individually — it takes cues from the leaders and authorities it already trusts. So don't persuade the crowd; persuade the crowd's trusted sources first, and let the endorsement flow downhill.*

**When:** Upstream of copy — deciding *who should say it* and *what event makes it news* before deciding what to say. Launches, PR, category creation, entering markets where the seller's own voice carries no trust.

**The four mechanisms:**

1. **Third-party authority** *(the load-bearing one)*. People delegate judgment to group leaders — physicians, scientists, institutions, editors, respected practitioners. "If you can influence the leaders, either with or without their conscious cooperation, you automatically influence the group which they sway" (*Propaganda*). Order of operations: map which authorities your prospect already defers to → earn genuine endorsement there → only then address the crowd. Archetype: the Beech-Nut bacon campaign — no bacon ads; a physician's survey of ~4,500 doctors endorsing a hearty breakfast, reported as news. The seller's voice never appeared; the trusted one did. Modern forms: the analyst report, the standards body, the benchmark maintainer, the respected practitioner's independent writeup.
2. **Address the group, not the individual.** People decide as members of overlapping groups whose approval they need; a message that moves the group identity beats one aimed at solitary reason. (The lab-verified fragments of this are Cialdini's Social Proof and Unity, above.)
3. **Sell the symbol, not the object.** Attach the product to an identity or cause the buyer already wants: cigarettes staged as "torches of freedom" in the 1929 Easter parade; Ivory soap sold to children as sculpting material; don't sell pianos — popularize the *music room* until the piano is its natural furniture. The purchase is the visible tip of an invisible desire; find the desire.
4. **Create circumstances; don't argue.** Stage an overt, newsworthy act that makes the conclusion arise from the world rather than from the seller — the event *is* the argument. Ancestor of the launch stunt, the commissioned study, the record-setting public demo. (Boorstin later named the degenerate form: the pseudo-event.)

**Watch out:**

- **Undisclosed fronts are now illegal and fatal.** Bernays' "independent" institutes and surveys were often quietly client-funded; today that is astroturfing (FTC disclosure rules). Being caught converts all borrowed authority into permanent distrust. The modern application is *earned, disclosed* endorsement — put the real thing in real experts' hands and let them speak freely.
- **His victories are self-reported.** Bernays was his own best client; the standard independent account (Larry Tye, *The Father of Spin*, 1998) confirms the campaigns happened but deflates the credit and numbers. Use the mechanisms; discount the legend.
- **The machinery has no ethics built in.** The same playbook sold tobacco to women (he later regretted it) and ran United Fruit's PR around the 1954 Guatemala coup; Goebbels reportedly kept his books (per Bernays' own memoir). The Ethics section below governs: authority real, endorsement freely given, interest disclosed.
- **Authority doesn't transfer across domains** (see Cialdini's Authority row) — a celebrity is not a physician.

> Maps to the canon: Cialdini's Authority / Social Proof / Unity are the individually lab-verified fragments; Berger's STEPPS explains why the story spreads *after* the event. Bernays' distinct, still-underused contribution is **sequence** — engineer the trusted-third-party layer *before* addressing the market. Most marketers shout at the crowd and skip its leaders. Both 1920s books are public domain — read them as primary sources on mechanism, adversarially on ethics.

### Neuromarketing / NeuroMap — Patrick Renvoisé & Christophe Morin, Neuromarketing (2002/2007) / The Persuasion Code (2018)

*A message-construction methodology built on one claim: the fast, automatic "primal brain" (their term; ≈ Kahneman's System 1) triggers the decision, and the rational brain justifies it afterward — so package every message to pass the primal brain's narrow filters, or the best offer never reaches the actual decider.*

**When:** Packaging a finished message — pitch, deck, landing page, demo, VSL — after the diagnosis/offer/positioning work is done. Cialdini gives you *which* trigger to pull; this gives you *how to encode and sequence* the whole message. Use it as a pre-send checklist and a structure for live presentations.

⚠️ **Science status, read first:** the "old brain / reptilian brain" framing rests on MacLean's triune-brain model, which modern neuroanatomy treats as a myth in its literal form (no independent reptilian decision module; brain regions are densely integrated). The authors' 2018 rebrand to "primal brain" + System 1/System 2 framing is the defensible core. Treat the anatomy as metaphor; the *craft* levers stand on their own — they converge with independently verified canon (Hopkins' specificity, PAS, Heath's SUCCESs: concrete, emotional, story, credible, simple).

**The six stimuli** — the primal brain's filters; run every message against all six (2018 relabels in parentheses):

| Stimulus | Filter | Apply it |
|---|---|---|
| **Self-centered** *(Personal)* | Only "what does this mean for *me*" gets through; sender-info is noise | Never open with "we/our company." Every claim rephrasable as something that happens *to the customer* |
| **Contrast** *(Contrastable)* | Without a before/after anchor, the default under ambiguity is *don't act* | Structure as explicit contrast: without X → [pain]; with X → [relief]. Differentiate against the status quo, not in a vacuum |
| **Tangible** | Abstractions require slow decoding; concrete input processes instantly | "Saves you 3 hours a week," not "synergistic value creation." Concrete nouns, exact numbers |
| **Beginning & end** *(Memorable)* | Attention peaks at the open and close (primacy/recency); the middle is a trough | Strongest claim in the opening seconds; close by repeating it. Never bury the ask mid-message |
| **Visual** | Visual cues process before language does | Show the pain state vs. relief state as an image, not a paragraph |
| **Emotion** *(Emotional)* | Emotional charge cements memory and triggers action; flat description doesn't | Reconnect them to the felt pain (story, vivid stakes) before offering relief |

**The four steps** (their whole method in sequence):

1. **Diagnose the Pain** — structured intake like a doctor's: source (root cause, not symptom), intensity (low-intensity pain doesn't buy), consequences if unresolved, and — non-negotiable — the prospect **acknowledges the diagnosis out loud** before you proceed. Pain comes in three types: financial, strategic, personal.
2. **Differentiate your Claims** — max **three** claims, each tied to a diagnosed pain, each passing **TOP**: **T**herapeutic (solves a real diagnosed pain), **O**riginal (a claim a competitor could equally make is free advertising for them), **P**rovable. Validate with real customers, don't assert.
3. **Demonstrate the Gain** — the primal brain is skeptical; prove gain in the same three types (financial/strategic/personal) using the proof hierarchy, strongest first: **customer case > live demo > data > vision**. Never lead with vision when you have a case.
4. **Deliver to the Old Brain** — package via the six building blocks below. Their point: this step gates everything; a logically airtight, well-proven message that isn't effortless to process still converts zero.

**The six message building blocks** (each exploits one stimulus):

| Block | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Grabber** (opens) | Seize the attention peak. Five types: **mini-drama** (a day in their life with the pain, then the same day with your solution), **wordplay**, **rhetorical question**, **prop** (a physical object standing for the value), **story** |
| **Claims ×3** | The three TOP claims, short and memorable, **repeated** throughout — repetition compensates for the primal brain's short, non-verbal attention |
| **Big Picture** | One visual carrying the value prop — ideally a contrasted pain-state/relief-state image pair — registered before any text is parsed |
| **Proofs of Gain** | The evidence hierarchy (case > demo > data > vision) across financial/strategic/personal gain |
| **Handling Objections** | Most late objections are disguised fear, not logic — resolve the emotion (restate, step into their side, give proof) instead of piling on more data |
| **Close** | Repeat the claims one final time, then two open questions: *"What do you think?"* and *"Where do we go from here?"* — surfaces residual objections without an adversarial hard ask |

**The seven impact boosters** (delivery layer, on top of content): word it with **"you"**; **be credible** (passion and integrity read as trust signals independent of content); **apply contrast** everywhere options could look interchangeable; **trigger emotion**; **vary the channel** (visual/auditory/kinesthetic — note: VAK learning-styles theory is itself weakly evidenced; use as variety heuristic only); **tell stories** (narrative is processed like lived experience); **less is more** (cut anything without direct customer value).

**Watch out:**

- **The neuroscience wrapper oversells.** Don't repeat "the reptilian brain decides" as fact in your own marketing-science claims; the tactical checklist works, the anatomy story doesn't.
- **Skipping pain acknowledgment** — claims anchored to a pain the prospect never confirmed land as generic assertions.
- **More than three claims** — dilutes all of them.
- **Leading with vision** when a customer case exists inverts the proof hierarchy and reads as hype.
- **Mini-drama grabbers** require real understanding of the prospect's day; a generic reenactment reads as theater.
- This is a *packaging* method — it cannot rescue an undiagnosed market, a weak offer, or absent positioning (steps 1–4 of the SKILL.md procedure come first).

> Maps to the canon: steps 1–3 ≈ Pain-Agitate-Solve made granular; the six stimuli overlap heavily with Heath's SUCCESs and Hopkins' specificity — independent convergence that the craft layer is sound. The authors run a consultancy (SalesBrain/NeuroMap™) and the books partly market it; weight the checklist, not the brand. See EXPERTS.md for the credibility entry.

## Ethics (non-negotiable)

Persuasion principles amplify trust — or destroy it. Use **real** scarcity, **real** social proof, **real** authority. Manufactured proof or faked independence is the line between a durable brand and an FTC consent order (Joe Sugarman, 1989). The honest application compounds across a career; the dishonest one is a one-time withdrawal against a reputation you don't get back. If a principle only "works" by misleading the prospect, it isn't working — it's borrowing against the future.
