# Clarity: the ground eloquence stands on

Distilled from Maxim Ilyakhov, *Ясно, понятно. Как доносить мысли и убеждать людей с помощью слов* ("Clear, understood: how to convey ideas and persuade people with words"; Alpina Publisher, 2021, ISBN 978-5-9614-3582-5). Ilyakhov is the editor behind *Пиши, сокращай*, the Tinkoff Journal and the Kod journal; this book is his second level, past cutting: context, interest, image, presentation. Each section names its source chapters in the original Russian, so they can be found in any edition. It is a distillation with citations, not a retelling; buy the book, the examples are the point.

Why it belongs in an eloquence skill: a resonant line that is misunderstood, mistimed, or about the author instead of the reader is noise with good rhythm. The masters in [canon.md](canon.md) reached beauty by perfecting truth, clarity and force first ([SKILL.md](../SKILL.md), the hierarchy). This file is the working method for the clarity rung, and the warning about what clarity can be used for.

> "Неважно, насколько ты был прав, если тебя не поняли." – It does not matter how right you were if you were not understood. («Проклятие умных людей»)

When people do not understand us it is not our fault, but it is our problem. "Being right is not enough. You also have to be effective" (Neil deGrasse Tyson, quoting his father).

---

## 1. Short is not clear

*«Коротко – не значит ясно»*

Cut garbage, add value. Garbage: words that carry no meaning, judgments where facts would do, the author's feelings in place of the subject, bureaucratese for a lay reader. Value, which must never be cut for brevity: the key theses, examples, anti-examples, the chain of explanation (backstory, story, conclusion), attention handles, illustrations. "Removing the examples is like putting three cutlets on a guest's plate and then taking two back".

His lawyer case: "Registering a trademark protects your exclusive right to a means of individualization from unscrupulous competitors" is dense, short and useless to the business owner it was written for. The clear version is three times longer: your nail salon "Ruby Power" moves; a new salon opens at the old address under the same name; your clients walk in there; a registered mark lets you stop it and claim compensation.

**Rule:** clear first, then short. When a client demands maximum compression, say: we can cut the examples, but then the reader will not understand, which defeats the task; here is what I can do instead; the decision is yours.

## 2. Context decides whether the line lands

*Part «Контекст»: «Как влияет контекст», «Как это выглядит», «Шаблоны», «Исправление контекста», «Последнее слово о контексте»*

Context is everything around the words: who speaks, the relationship and trust between speaker and reader, where it appears and what the reader expects there, when (what is happening in the world today), the reader's state, the tone and the look. Context is largely irrational: people do not think a blogger is bought, they feel it.

- **Same words, opposite results.** "Ate and lost 10 kg in three months" on a banner is filtered out; the same claim from a classmate who visibly lost weight converts. The text was identical; the context was not.
- **A good line in a broken context becomes infamous.** "Вы держитесь здесь! Вам всего доброго, хорошего настроения и здоровья!" ("Hang in there! All the best, good mood and health to you!") is a pleasant sentence. Said by a prime minister inside a ring of guards to pensioners whose pensions were not indexed, it entered political history as mockery. Before polishing a line, check who will say it, to whom, and on what day.
- **Templates carry their original context.** The shock opening, the personal-drama arc, the "buy now" push each worked somewhere. The Instagram single-mother redemption template, transplanted to enterprise software, reads as grotesque. "Не принимайте на веру никакие шаблоны" – trust no template.
- **Lively or calm is a context choice.** Dense subheads and pull-quotes say "I care whether you read this"; a calm, unbroken essay (his example: Paul Graham's *What You Can't Say*) says "read carefully or leave." Use calm for an expensive program that needs attentive people, lively for a mass free service.
- **Certainty is a tone with a price.** Disclaimers ("these are my personal observations") defuse attacks but sound unsure; the unconditional tone (his example: Artemy Lebedev) makes a small subject feel weighty and impresses novices, and provokes resistance in experts.

### Repairing a bad context: unpack the baggage

If something affects the reader's decision and everyone knows it but nobody says it, say it, plainly and with understanding (*выложить багаж*):

> "You are probably thinking: what can this young man teach us? He has lived less time than you have worked in journalism. And you are right." – Ilyakhov opening a workshop for veteran corporate journalists

> "A year ago I promised you… It is now clear the promise will not come true. You may think I deceived you."

Delivery matters: said calmly, low and unhurried, it builds trust; rattled off nervously, it reads as squirming. The move is different from the brand device *Weaponized Admission* in [craft.md](craft.md): that flips a weakness into proof of virtue; this one only names the elephant so the conversation can begin. If the bad context is your own behavior, the repair is an apology to each person before any message (his story of a colleague who shouted; his own story of sarcastic editing comments, where a well-written company-wide email changed nothing).

### Sometimes the text is not the lever

"Иногда мы прилагаем усилия туда, куда можем, а не туда, где будет результат" – sometimes we put effort where we can, not where the result is. A company that owes suppliers millions polishes the perfect letter for days, when the director has to call each supplier in person. A marketer and a copywriter agonize over a slogan instead of talking to buyers. Before making a line sing, ask whether words are the instrument at all.

And sometimes say nothing. On the day the world is grieving, one day of silence beats a campaign.

## 3. Interest: the reader reads about themselves

*Part «Интерес»: «Великое школьное искажение», «Главный секрет внимания», «Полезное действие для читателя», «Всё о себе да о себе»*

**The great school distortion**. At school the teacher opens every essay, reads it to the end, and grades spelling. In life nobody is obliged to open the text, the reader skims, and they came for meaning, not form. The adult residue: "if I wrote it, they will read it"; "there is a correct format"; "a literate text is a good text." Hence the meeting where grown people spend hours choosing between two mission phrasings that differ by one word. Nobody cares what you strive for. Write the mission however you like, at the end; solve the reader's task in the first paragraph.

This is aimed squarely at manifestos, mission statements and about-pages, the core use of this skill. Run the test below before any figure of speech.

**The main secret of attention**: a person is interested in themselves, wants to read about themselves and to speak about themselves. Compare two year-end summaries: "10 million lines of code, 2,000 liters of coffee, 38 articles" versus "our clients broadcast from 180 countries; if you watched the Olympics, the picture and sound probably reached you through our…" The second is about the reader and lets every client feel part of something significant.

**Fly out of your body**. In a metro car, pick a stranger and reconstruct their view of the room, the weight of their backpack, where they are going, what their evening looks like. The point is the moment you feel yourself become flat background in their film. If you are background, your company and its manifesto are even more so.

**Useful action**: why a reader would choose to give attention:

| Kind | Promise | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Pragmatic | solves a problem | works only if the reader knows the problem exists, considers it important, and is ready to solve it now or soon |
| Social | makes them more loved, respected, belonging | "what's in the backpack of the founder I admire"; local pride; sharing it makes me look good |
| Emotional | curiosity, fun, intrigue | the level of emotion is a context decision |

A resonant line usually carries the social or emotional action; it still has to carry one.

**Reflect, do not lecture**. A title has to mirror what the reader already believes and wants; the place to change their mind is inside, after they have chosen to listen. "The topic is not the place to teach the reader how to live." His example: an article about negotiating a raise for readers who fear the word "negotiation" is titled for what they want ("how to squeeze the most money out of your company"), and inside it shows them that honest negotiation is the way.

**Write in their words.** People who need a text on self-pressure do not think "I pressure myself"; they think "I'm burned out." Title it with their word, then reframe inside. And do not claim knowledge of their life you lack: "your competitors never sleep" sent to a tiny, friendly niche is laughable; a video telling cartridge resellers how much they earn per cartridge was cut, because "if you're not the expert, don't climb into their head". Describing the reader's reality with hidden contempt leaks through, and the reader feels it.

**Ban the word "inform."** A text cannot have the task of informing, showing, demonstrating, highlighting or telling. The moment someone says it, a dead text is being planned. Replace it with what changes for the reader.

### The virus of rightness

Arguments about being right are the strongest attention magnet in existence and the worst place to change anyone's mind. Each side's picture of the world comes from lived experience, so each is right from where it stands. To move someone without triggering it:

1. Find the generalization the reader will defend ("all clients are stingy").
2. Show it holds under certain conditions, and that other conditions exist where it does not ("on Instagram, yes, 20–30 kopecks a character; banks, telecoms and online schools pay differently").
3. Focus on the path from their state to the one you want.

The opposite move, attacking their worldview with confidence and a discredited enemy, wins the onlookers and loses the people addressed. "Я хочу быть правым или услышанным?" – do I want to be right, or heard? "Вирус правоты – это когда тебе важнее быть правым, чем успешным" – the virus of rightness is when being right matters more to you than succeeding.

### The language of war

Words like *enemy, war, fight, traitor, Judas, parasite, treacherous, humiliating, monstrous, barbaric, on our knees, crush, destroy, trample*, and their noble twins *pride, strength, dignity, honor, justice, duty, Motherland, defense, security, solidarity, order* – when a text stacks them, someone wants extra emotion from you. It paints another person as an enemy, a non-person, and "as soon as a person moves into the class of enemies, you may do to them everything you normally may not". The rational discussion is over; agitation has begun.

This is the eloquence trap: the Churchillian register is built from exactly these words. Reserve it for a real enemy and a real defense, and know that using it ends argument.

## 4. Text: the reader must see a film

*Part «Текст»: «Передача информации» through «Не мусольте текст»; Part «Подача»: «Что мы говорим и что показываем»*

"Мы не информируем читателя. Мы помогаем ему разобраться" – we don't inform the reader, we help them figure it out. To understand something is to hold a whole image of it: its parts, causes and logic. Facts without that image are white noise; a dubious but coherent hypothesis ("the oprichnina was a giant trading enterprise run by the tsar") produces more understanding than a page of verified dates. Hence the governing rule:

> "Чтобы человек смог разобраться в нашей идее, он должен увидеть в голове что-то вроде художественного фильма." – For a person to grasp our idea, they must see something like a feature film in their head.

"Be careful when signing a contract" is logically correct and lights up nothing. To make it light up: examples, anti-examples, the reader kept "OK", familiar circumstances, a metaphor, a branded rule, an illustration, walking them through it, from several sides.

### Abstraction + example + anti-example

- **Both halves.** "An example is needed so we understand something; the abstraction, so we understand exactly what is needed." An example with no thesis crumbles ("an example of what?"); a thesis with no example is forgotten within a minute.
- **"Если к мысли нет примера, эта мысль не нужна"** – if a thought has no example, the thought is not needed. He calls a good example 80% of the work on a text: the theses of *Пиши, сокращай* fit on two pages; the other 438 are examples. This is why every digest of a good book fails.
- **Three criteria for an example:** it provokes no resistance; the reader recognizes themselves; it has no logical or factual errors. It must unpack the thesis's own terms, not borrow a famous company: a thesis followed by Steve Jobs and the iPad is "shoving Jobs in our face".
- **One example world.** Pick one situation (one flat purchase, one persona named Oleg) and carry it through, so the reader enters the world once. "Ah, that's the chapter where we bought a computer!" is the result to want. Announce the domain before an example and again when switching ("now a case from manufacturing"); an example that arrives from nowhere jolts the reader.
- **Detail only on purpose.** A pile of vivid particulars hypnotizes and the reader loses the thread. "Не топите читателя в подробностях просто так. Топите осознанно!" – don't drown the reader in details for nothing; drown them deliberately. This is the clarity-side version of the catalogue in [craft.md](craft.md): accumulation is a device only when it is chosen.
- **The anti-example answers "so how is it wrong?"** Advice is only needed where people already act otherwise, so the wrong way is currently more convenient, and those conditions will still be there after reading. "Люди действуют не так, как им сказали… Люди действуют так, как они привыкли" – people act not as told but as they are used to. Each anti-example visits their "my case is different" in advance. "Экспертность проявляется во владении примерами, антипримерами и нюансами. Это всё" – expertise is command of examples, anti-examples and nuances; that is all. And: anti-examples are always more interesting than examples.
- **Thesis against its inverse.** A vague value lands when set against the world it replaces: "Раньше терпел, теперь улыбайся" – before: endure; now: smile. The antithesis in [craft.md](craft.md), built from concrete opposites.
- **Start from the example, not the topic.** "Attaching a thesis to a good example takes two minutes. The reverse doesn't work". Real examples come from work, clients, the audience; invented ones fail the reality check.

### "Не" leaves its picture

The copywriters' rule that "people don't perceive negation" is, in his words, complete nonsense. The real point: the reader cannot help seeing what you draw, negated or not. "Не влезай – убьёт" (keep out, it kills) works precisely because it leaves the picture "went in, got killed". Test a line by the scene it plants, not by its grammar. "Don't think of an elephant" fails; "Keep out, it kills" succeeds; both contain a negation.

### The reader is always OK

"Что бы ни случилось, читатель не виноват" – whatever happened, the reader is not to blame.

- Excuse the error by finding the view in which it isn't shameful: good motives, no way out, misled or pushed by a third party. "There are moments when resisting the cake is very hard, even with an iron will" instead of "if you lack willpower…".
- Explain mistakes by circumstance, not stupidity: "that's from school; he has a distorted idea of business language", not "he writes like a robot out of stupidity."
- Pronouns, all else equal: the good things go to "you", the bad to "he/they", the contested to "we" (he flags this as a hunch, not a proven effect).
- No in-group enemy made of the people you want to win: "a good editor knows…", "there is a category of copywriters who can't…". His youthful attacks reached only his own side: "all because I was saying: you're bad."
- To win a hostile room, open by sincerely honoring it: "Old-school journalists are heroes in my eyes. But modern society…".
- "Есть правота – и есть эффективность" – there is being right, and there is being effective. An article is an invitation, not a consultation someone asked for; lecturing from above makes the guest leave.

### Banal advice, from the other side

"Brush your teeth in the evening" is true and changes nothing. Write about why people don't do it and what stops them: problem → everyone knows the solution in theory → few do it in practice → *why? what's in the way?* Focus the piece on the last step, and iterate the why (late for the airport → packing left to the last minute → packing takes mental effort → fix it with a checklist made in advance). Reassure while you do it: packing really is hard; you are not obliged to be an expert.

### Person + does + this way

Real heroes and action verbs light up a picture; abstractions interacting with verbal nouns do not.

- "The degree of a consultant's attentiveness is the determining factor of sales growth" → "The more carefully a consultant answers questions, the more often customers come back."
- State verbs (*is, serves as, is located, exists, there is*) are a red flag: "Our main advantage is the promptness of our responses" → "We answer around the clock: operators are on the line even on New Year's Eve."
- The formula **"человек + делает + так"** (person + does + this way): not "rehearsals take place" but "actors rehearse."
- Choose whose face the reader sees: "The builders built the houses on time" puts builders in the frame; "The houses are ready. The first owners have their keys and are already renovating" puts the reader's future in it. "It's less about grammar than about directing the reader's imagination."
- Once the picture is drawn, judgments and new images are allowed.

### Circumstances, numbers, and the main thing close together

- **Circumstances:** concrete enough to imagine, familiar, prepared for, and not stealing attention. Replace class nouns (*measures, events, products, solutions, opportunities, various*) with named things: "various events will be organized" → "a tour of the Kremlin, dinner at a restaurant, a boat ride." And make them true to the reader's life: a 22-year-old with her own Moscow flat advising readers to save a third of their income becomes the topic instead of the advice.
- **Facts over judgments, and every fact anchored**: by comparison (Russia's 2017 oil and gas revenue of 5.8 trillion roubles against 8.2 trillion paid out by the Pension Fund and the most expensive yacht at 0.3 trillion; ten billion and ten trillion feel the same until compared) or by a scenario of use: "Mom streams 4K. The kid streams 4K. Dad watches sport in 4K. The cat Skypes in 4K. Nothing lags." Round when the text is for understanding, not accounting: 32.5% is "every third", 28 minutes is "half an hour", 985,784,090 is "a billion." "Делайте читателю легко" – make it easy for the reader.
- **Selected facts can produce any emotion.** "There is no truth here: only selection and juxtaposition of facts". Against a biased reader, facts bounce: "show you came in good faith; make them want to listen; only then, facts".
- **Main thing close, details apart**: keep subject and verb together, put the point in the first sentence with nothing else in it. A 60-word safety instruction becomes "In an emergency, leave your workplace." What must the reader remember? "Emergency: leave." Give each important nuance its own sentence; a "nearly always" buried mid-clause is not read.
- **Don't chew the text**: the polishing author is concentrated; the reader skims. Spend effort in the order the reader reads: headings and paragraph starts, then examples and illustrations, nuances last. "Всё, что я делаю ближе к концу абзаца, скорее всего, будет утрачено" – whatever I do near the end of a paragraph will most likely be lost. For eloquence this is a placement rule: the line that must survive goes where the eye lands.

### Say what passes a logic check; show what must be felt

Ilyakhov's hypothesis, which he labels unproven: "Everything the reader should think, express in text, numbers, tables and lists. Everything the reader should feel, express in images." A written emotional promise switches on the reader's reason, which compares it with their life and objects. "With this backpack you can go to any country in the world" draws "I don't even have a passport." "You'll shine at any party" draws "how do you know?" The honest fix keeps the verifiable in words (two recorded tracks, access to the alumni chat, an invitation to play at the showcase concert) and lets photographs carry the feeling.

For this skill, the consequence: a resonant line must still pass the logic check. Promise in words only what is true and checkable; when the feeling is the point, render it as a concrete image the reader sees for themselves (the shoes in Didion, the turtle in its plastic corset), never as a claim about what they will feel.

## 5. Metaphor: the clearest tool and the most dangerous

*«Аналогии и метафоры»*

Analogies and metaphors make text clear and vivid; sometimes so strongly that they distract from the point and trap the author.

- **The reader imports properties you did not mean.** "A broker is like a waiter: you order, he places it in the kitchen, brings the dish, you pay through him and leave a tip." But brokers' fees are not voluntary tips; a kitchen always cooks your dish, an exchange may not fill your order; a fake restaurant is easy to spot, fake brokers outnumber real ones. The reader is not merely clear but *too* clear: a false sense of understanding that leads to trusting a fake broker or losing everything.
- **The author gets trapped.** Ilyakhov taught that stop-words are the fat of a text; then extended it to trimming fat off a steak; a student noted that fat is what makes meat taste good. Ten minutes of arguing inside his own metaphor.
- **Demagogues run on metaphor.** "Poverty is a disease, and I have the cure" implies a simple remedy for a many-caused condition. "You cannot fill a vessel that is already full" builds an education argument on an invented model of the brain. His favorite bad one: "A human is 70% water, so your text should be 70% water too." "При должном желании метафорой можно объяснить любую чушь" – with enough will, a metaphor can explain any nonsense.
- **An elegant analogy hides its gaps, and only another image can answer it.** Lebedev's "choose your wife by tender, then replace her every four years" (against procurement rules and term limits) cannot be beaten by "political competition builds institutions", which is "professorial mumbling" with no image in it. It can be beaten only by a counter-image: "term limits are the option to divorce; a spouse who knows you can't leave has no reason to improve".
- **When metaphors are safe:** when they sit close to reality (in a music-software book, the instrument area is the *orchestra pit*, clips are *sheet music*, you are the *conductor*), or when they serve one narrow goal and their inaccuracies do not touch any decision the reader will make (a ping-pong ball bouncing around a bare room versus sinking into carpets explains reverb; physically wrong, practically enough).

**The rule:** "Они не плохи сами по себе, но не должны лежать в основе вашего текста" – metaphors are not bad in themselves, but they must not be the foundation of your text. That is the rule for explanation and persuasion. In a poem, the governing metaphor (Rumi's reed, [craft.md](craft.md)) *is* the experience; the moment a metaphor carries an argument the reader will act on, audit it.

**Divergence audit** (derived from his examples): list three to five properties of the source image. For each, ask whether the target shares it. If an unshared property touches money, health, safety or trust, drop the metaphor or fence it in the next sentence ("unlike a waiter, the broker's fee is fixed and not optional").

## 6. Plot: empathy is the glue

*«Сюжет»*

Reality is chaos; facts alone are white noise. A plot selects facts and glues them into a picture a person can hold. "Elon Musk beat Rogozin" is a plot; "thousands of engineers at a company working on government contracts, standing on Soviet and American predecessors" is reality and nobody's story.

- **The operative ingredient is empathy, not the textbook list.** Hero, antagonist, conflict, resolution, transformation, understandable circumstances: that list is in every book on drama. Ilyakhov's claim (he flags it as unproven): plot begins where we can put ourselves in the heroes' place and feel what they feel, triumph with one, shame with the other. Good and evil are optional.
- **Plot gives importance.** "Ocean plastic pollution is one of the important environmental problems" goes in one ear and out the other. A turtle found in 2018 with its shell pinched in the middle like a corset, because as a hatchling it got caught in a plastic six-pack ring, does not.
- **Make the reader the agent of justice.** His guide to a marketplace scam ends with "report the link and we'll bring in the police." Without the reader winning, the plot is too weak.
- **Pick circumstances the reader knows.** The scam example uses an iPhone, not a vintage AKAI MPC60 sampler; if the specific thing matters, first give the general idea ("rare, no longer sold, museum pieces"), then the name. A story set in "moving out from my parents" reaches more people than one set in "emigrating to Riga".
- **In advertising, a fact becomes a scenario.** "Quieter than a running fridge" → "run it day and night; it won't wake you or the neighbors" → "you can put it in the nursery." Each fact, glued into a moment of the reader's life.
- **Every plot is a simplification, a fiction, sometimes a lie.** The same facts about Catherine II's Legislative Commission make three plots: a brilliant PR operator, a naive idealist, a usurper buying legitimacy. To build a plot you must attribute motives and feelings you cannot know: the history-textbook dilemma. Propaganda lays motives on thick. The gum commercial implies the contract was won by chewing gum: false causality felt as true.
- **Storytelling is a tool, not a goal.** It persuades best about people, behavior, relationships. "Storytelling in an article on quantum mechanics looks dragged in by the ears".

## 7. Slogans and coined terms

*«Слоганы и собственные термины»*

A slogan is a saying in an unusual, memorable linguistic form: a set tempo, a clear rhythm, unusual drama. The mind enjoys such play and absorbs it. Children's rhymes and political slogans are the same species.

- **Rhythm can beat precision.** In a book on making music Ilyakhov wrote "Искусство свободно" (art is free): rhythmic, clear, it became his lecture line. "Творчество свободно" (creativity is free) was closer to what they meant and would not have stuck. "Пиши, сокращай" (write, cut) has the inner rhythm of internet folklore. "Человек + делает + так" (person + does + this way) is remembered by students; "тезис, пример, антипример" (thesis, example, anti-example), same weight of content, is not. In this skill's hierarchy rhythm may decide only between candidates that are both true.
- **Fold your principles into a song or a verse.** "Лучше никакого слогана, чем вялый и неестественный" – better no slogan than a limp, unnatural one.
- **Name the thing, and it starts to exist.** "Явления не существуют, если у них нет названия" – phenomena do not exist if they have no name. Bureau Gorbunov's terms (*infostyle, stop-words, the newcomer's eye, unmovable deadline*) became the trade's vocabulary and mark who belongs. His wife's term *magnolia* (a tender, fragile, infantile colleague who cannot do useful work and complains publicly) made the behavior shameful once it had a name.
- **People respond to the status a name confers, not to the content.** SWOT is four plain questions; the 4P "marketing mix" is four plain questions; named, they became points of support. "Людям не нужны «приемы редакторской правки для повышения информативности». Людям нужен инфостиль." – people don't need "editing techniques to increase informativeness"; people need infostyle.
- **The same mechanism inflates nonsense.** Call any dilettante's trading tips "the Ilyakhov method" and it gains weight; prefix anything with "crypto" or "neuro." Coin a term only for something real and useful; the name travels further than your caveats.

## 8. Repetition across time

*«Сила повторения»*

A text is training, not transmission; some views change after one reading, most need to ripen.

- **Almost nobody saw it the first time.** His working estimate from his own channels: under 1% of subscribers see a post in the first hour, about 10% within the day, another 15% within the week; a small share reaches the end; fewer remember. A month later most people do not notice a repeat.
- **Repeat four ways:** at different times, across several pieces; from different sides, with different situations, examples and arguments; from different sources, building a sense of consensus; without visible pressure, watching the context.
- **The eleventh example lands.** Readers who did not recognize themselves in ten examples recognize the eleventh and say "where were you before?" On his bank training, the same principle shown on the bank's own documents made everyone photograph the screen, though he had shown it the day before on other examples.
- **Tempo is a tool.** A slow book walks the reader by the hand and repeats; flashcards fire theses like a machine gun. Choose for the reader you are picturing, knowing the fast reader will call the slow book repetitive.

The refrain in [craft.md](craft.md) repeats the thesis within one piece. This is the other half: a line becomes immortal by being said for years from many angles, not by being said perfectly once.

## 9. Order of explanation

*«Порядок объяснения», «С чего начать статью»*

The default that works in most cases:

1. **Context and interest** – is the reader willing to hear this, from you, now?
2. **Introduction to the situation** – one or two sentences that load every key theme, "like an overture": "If you exercise irregularly, runners may puzzle you: how do they get out for a run two or three times a week, when any workout is a feat for you?"
3. **Thesis**, or a gradual approach to it when the subject is complex.
4. **Examples and anti-examples** – Igor started at 6 km/h and now holds 9 for an hour; Viktor sprints at 14 km/h along the main avenue, once a month, because he is exhausted.
5. **Proof** – research and mechanism, if the examples have not already convinced.

Variants: build everything around one example (a beginner's first run, on which his whole sporting future depends; works only if that one case contains every circumstance you need); an imagined world that detaches the reader from triggers ("a village of 100 people on an island" to explain taxes without summoning the pension reform); one big analogy at the heart ("you've roasted 50 kg of sunflower seeds and taken them to market" to explain an exchange; run the divergence audit).

**Openings**: write any introduction, delete it, start from the second paragraph. Or: useful headline; why this author can be trusted; what the reader gets; how the text is organized. About 80% of readers skip introductions anyway.

**Where to learn explaining:** science documentaries, written for novices and onlookers: Carl Sagan's *Cosmos* and its sequel with Neil deGrasse Tyson (both co-written by Ann Druyan), Walter Lewin's physics lectures, Harvard's CS50, PBS *Space Time*, Vsauce. When one pulls you in, stop and work out what lit the interest.

**The magic mirror:** try a device on real people, watch their faces or comments, note what worked for which subject. Mastery accumulates from that, not from a technique.

## 10. The warning: clarity is not truth

*«Проклятие умных людей», «Манипуляция ясностью», «Обманчивая ясность»*

The book opens and closes on this, and so should any use of this skill.

- **The same tools sell a pyramid.** Right after the analyst's story about not putting all your eggs in one basket, Ilyakhov tells a second, equally clear story: a junior colleague bought a new Audi by doubling bets on sports with borrowed money; "it's maths!" A photo of the car, consistent examples, anti-examples, hand-holding, a metaphor, a friendly context, "and here is a ruined family". MMM's Lyonya Golubkov chart of family prosperity was a clarity tool too.
- **Clarity without facts.** A leaflet: the management company's director is a party to dozens of lawsuits and rides a motorcycle worth millions; don't let crooks run our building. No facts, yet "everything is clear." The lawsuits were against contractors on behalf of residents.
- **The picture contradicts the warning.** A broker's article says plainly that stocks are risky, and illustrates the risk with charts whose windows always start low and end high. Legally correct; the heart reads "my stocks will multiply".
- **"Ясность и очевидность – это не признак правды. Это результат того, что поработали профессионалы."** Clarity and obviousness are not a sign of truth; they are the result of professionals at work.
- **Every clarity is a simplification.** When something becomes clear to someone, they have dropped, excluded, or not noticed something. "Ясно" is one angle out of infinitely many. "The feeling of clarity is deceptive, but we have nothing else" – so we choose the angle and which changes to serve.

For this skill that settles the order: the hierarchy in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) puts truth first because every device in this file and in [craft.md](craft.md) works equally well for a lie. Aim them only at what is true and serves the listener, and when you see them aimed at you, read the axes.
