# Structural moves

These are rhetorical shapes, not words — they survive synonym substitution, which is why
rewriting them "in other words" fails. Fix the move, not its surface.

## 1. False agency
Inanimate or abstract subject performing a human action: "the data tells us", "the
decision emerged", "a complaint becomes a fix", "the culture shifted", "the market
rewards", "this essay argues".
**Fix:** name the human. If no specific actor fits, use "you". ("We read the data and
found…", "The board decided…")
**Exempt:** established technical idiom ("the function returns", "the server accepts
connections"); personification as a deliberate, sustained device.

## 2. Binary contrast ("not X but Y") — the signature move
The manufactured-reversal family. Surface forms include:

- "It's not X. It's Y." / "It's not just X — it's Y."
- "X isn't the problem. Y is."
- "The answer isn't X. It's Y."
- "This isn't about X; it's about Y."
- "Not because X. Because Y."
- "not only X but also Y"

**Triage — three cases, three different fixes:**
1. *Strawman negation* (nobody actually claims X): delete X, assert Y directly.
2. *Real contrast* (someone holds X): name who holds X, and say concretely why Y beats
   it.
3. *Decorative* (X and Y are near-synonyms; the reversal exists for rhythm): delete the
   whole sentence.

**Banned escape hatches** — paraphrases that preserve the move and count as *new
findings*, not fixes:
- "less about X than Y"
- "X matters, but Y matters more"
- "the real X is Y"
- "the question isn't X, it's Y"
- "X? Y." (rhetorical-question variant)
- "— not X, but Y" (dash variant)

**Exempt:** a genuine correction of a stated, attributed position.

## 3. Negative listing (rhetorical striptease)
"Not a tool. Not a framework. A philosophy." / "No fluff. No filler. Just results."
**Fix:** say what it is, once. The teasing negations carry no information.

## 4. Dramatic fragmentation and manufactured punchlines
"X. That's it. That's the product." · staccato runs ("It had no preference. No
aesthetic. No nostalgia.") · mic-drop closers · "Full stop." / "Period." / "Let that
sink in."
**Fix:** merge fragments into real sentences; delete the drum-roll phrases. If the point
is strong, it doesn't need percussion.
**Exempt:** one deliberate fragment at a genuine climax, in a voice that uses them.

## 5. Colon reveal
Noun phrase, colon, lowercase dramatic payoff: "The result: chaos." "One problem:
nobody asked."
**Fix:** a plain sentence ("Nobody had asked for it").
**Exempt:** technical usage (label: value) and headlines in registers that use it.

## 6. Rule of three
Forced triads: "innovation, inspiration, and industry insights" — three items where the
third is padding, or three parallel clauses for rhythm.
**Fix:** keep the strongest item or two; cut the padding. If all three genuinely carry
distinct information, keep the content but break the rhythm.
**Exempt:** real tricolons where each item is load-bearing ("life, liberty, and the
pursuit of happiness").

## 7. Rhetorical setups
"Think about it:", "Here's what I mean:", "What if I told you…", "Consider this:",
"Picture this:".
**Fix:** delete the setup; the example or claim stands on its own.

## 8. Narrator from a distance
Armchair-sociologist voice: "Nobody designed this.", "People tend to…", "We've all
been there.", "Most people don't realize…"
**Fix:** put the reader in the scene — "you" beats "people" ("You don't sit down one
day and decide to…"). Or name the actual group observed.
**Exempt:** genuine survey/aggregate claims with a source.

## 9. Fake-profound kickers and aphorism formulas
"X is the Y of Z" · "X is not a tool but a mirror" · "the language of", "the currency
of" · any closing line that sounds like it wants to be quoted.
**Fix:** **delete — never re-metaphor.** Replacing one purple line with a better
metaphor reproduces the disease. End on the last real point instead.
**Exempt:** an aphorism the author wrote deliberately and would defend; quoted material.

## 10. Throat-clearing openers
"Here's the thing:", "Let me be clear:", "The uncomfortable truth is", "It turns out",
"I'll say it again:", "Can we talk about…"
**Fix:** delete; start with the claim.

## 11. Both-sidesing
Reflexive "on the other hand" neutrality where the piece exists to take a position;
hedged conclusions that refuse to land.
**Fix:** commit to the stated opinion. Keep a counterpoint only if it is genuinely
conceded, with its own weight.
**Exempt:** genuinely balanced surveys whose purpose is the survey.

## 12. Uniform rhythm
Every sentence the same length; every paragraph the same 3-sentence shape; every
section the same internal outline; every paragraph ending punchily.
**Fix:** vary by content — a long sentence where the thought is long, a short one where
it's short. **Warning:** mechanical long/short alternation is its own tell; so is
uniform "burstiness". Statistical thresholds are in [checks.md](checks.md).
