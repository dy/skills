# Voice: protect, calibrate, don't overcorrect

Subtraction alone produces beige prose — clean, voiceless, and just as recognizable as
slop. This file governs what survives the edit.

## Step 0: capture voice signals

Before editing, read the whole piece and write down 3–5 signals to protect:

- vocabulary level (says "stuff", or says "elements"?)
- cadence (short and blunt? long and winding? where does it break?)
- bluntness / politeness balance
- humor, irony, edge — and where it's deployed
- how uncertainty is expressed (hedges? confident guesses? "I don't know"?)
- digressions and asides — are they part of the voice?
- punctuation habits (parentheticals? semicolons? dashes?)

These survive the edit even when they resemble patterns from the rule files. A writer
who genuinely uses dashes keeps their dashes.

## Writing-sample calibration

If the user supplies a sample of their own writing, it **overrides every default in
this skill**. Extract from the sample: sentence-length distribution, paragraph openers,
punctuation habits (including dash frequency), recurring phrases and tics, transition
style, register. Match those in the rewrite. Don't upgrade "stuff" to "components";
don't produce long flowing sentences for a blunt writer.

## Signs of human writing — preserve, never "fix"

- Hard-to-fabricate specific detail ("ran into this on a 2019 Outback") — the single
  strongest human marker. Never fabricate one to fake humanity.
- Mixed or unresolved feelings; positions held with visible tension.
- First-person editorial choices the author could defend.
- Self-interrupting asides and genuine tangents.
- Dated slang, regionalisms, mild inconsistency in mechanics.
- Claims tied to named sources.

## What NOT to flag (false positives)

- Perfect grammar alone. Humans proofread.
- A single em dash, one "however", one "serves as" in formal register.
- Mixed registers — humans code-switch mid-piece.
- Genuine tricolons where all three items are load-bearing.
- Term-definition bullets in documentation.
- A question opener in genuine dialogue with the reader.
- Unsourced claims alone — humans assert without citing too.
- Negation by itself. "Don't" and "not" are not tells; the question is what picture the
  line leaves. "Keep out, it kills" works because it leaves "went in, got killed"
  (Ilyakhov). Only the manufactured reversal ("not X but Y") is a pattern.
- Long passages made of examples and anti-examples. Length that carries a picture is not
  padding.
- Title Case under a house style; curly quotes in typeset prose.
- Anything written before December 2022.

One pattern is a coincidence. Slop is convergence — several patterns co-occurring in
text with no voice signals.

## Register guide

Different rooms, different fatal tells, different legitimate structures.

| Register | Fatal tells | Legitimate there |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Academic** | reflexive hedging on settled points; "delve"; significance inflation in the abstract | calibrated hedges on contested claims; passive in methods sections |
| **Docs / README** | marketing adjectives; emoji walls; "blazingly fast"; boilerplate mission statements | term-definition bullets; imperative mood; repetition of exact names; brevity over voice |
| **Blog / essay** | throat-clearing; fake-profound kickers; recap endings | opinions, first person, one deliberate fragment, tangents |
| **Marketing copy** | invented metrics; "supercharge"; adjective piles | urgency and claims — when concrete and true; specificity sells better than superlatives |
| **Email** | signposting; padding; three paragraphs before the ask | plain short sentences; one clear ask |
| **Twitter/X** | hashtag spam; engagement bait; thread-announcing ("🧵👇") | compression, fragments, lowercase |
| **Reddit** | **any** bold in a comment; listicle structure; "Great question!" | concrete personal detail; casual mechanics; abrupt endings |
| **LinkedIn** | negative parallelism's natural habitat — scan twice; broetry line breaks | first-person work stories with real numbers |

## Soul — scoped to opinion writing only

For blog/essay/opinion contexts (never encyclopedic, technical, or legal text): a piece
can pass every pattern check and still read as generated because nobody is home. Signs:
every sentence the same temperature, no opinions, no uncertainty, no first person, no
humor. Fixes:

- Have reactions, not just reports ("I keep coming back to…" beats a neutral pro/con
  list).
- Acknowledge complexity honestly ("impressive and kind of unsettling").
- Be specific about feelings — not "this is concerning" but what specifically unsettles.
- Let some mess in: a tangent, an aside, a thought that doesn't fully resolve.

## Overcorrection is slop

The failure mode of deslop tools is a second recognizable genre: aggressively casual,
fake-humble, typo-sprinkled "humanized" text. Never:

- add fake typos or forced slang;
- insert "I" where the register doesn't carry a person;
- mechanically alternate long/short sentences;
- replace one purple metaphor with another (delete instead);
- fabricate personal anecdotes or concrete details;
- scrub so hard the author's actual voice goes with the slop.

When in doubt between a clever fix and a plain declarative sentence, choose the plain
sentence.
