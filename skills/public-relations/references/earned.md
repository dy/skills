# Earned — how coverage actually happens

For tactical B2B and startup journalist pitching, `coreyhaines31/marketingskills` → `public-relations` is already excellent and shouldn't be rebuilt. This file covers what that skill doesn't: how the media system actually works, what the survey and content-analysis data say, the placement decisions, and honest measurement.

## The system you are pitching into

**A large share of what you read as news originated as PR material.** The Cardiff study commissioned for *Flat Earth News* coded two constructed weeks of UK national news: roughly 60% of press stories were wholly or mainly wire copy and/or PR material, with about another 20% containing clear elements of it, and only around 12% verifiably originating with the reporter. The narrower measure — stories derived mainly or wholly from **PR material specifically** — was about 19%. Cite that 19% when you mean PR; the 60% figure combines wire copy with PR and is routinely misquoted.

Davies' term for the result is **churnalism**: under-resourced newsrooms passing material through with little independent checking. In the same data, only about 5% of articles had claims checked against another source at all.

**What this means for you, in two directions.** Practically, a genuinely useful, accurate, well-packaged story has an easier path than the romantic view of journalism suggests. Ethically, that same slack is exactly what Holiday exploited in *Trust Me, I'm Lying* — his "trading up the chain" works by planting on a low-standards outlet, then letting each tier treat the previous tier's pickup as verification. The pipeline's weakness is not a loophole to use. It's the reason your material has to be true, because nobody downstream will catch you and the correction, when it comes, will come from someone hostile.

## What journalists actually say they want

From the annual Cision and Muck Rack practitioner surveys (roughly 900–1,900 respondents each):

- They receive **50+ pitches a week**, and most reject pitches for **lack of relevance to their beat** — the single most-cited criterion.
- The stated ideal pitch is **under 200 words, sent before noon, with one timely follow-up**. Most prefer a 1:1 email over anything else.
- Roughly two-thirds rely on PR-provided material for story ideas, and a large majority say some of their published work began with a pitch — yet a substantial share also say genuinely beat-relevant pitches are *rare*. That gap is your entire opportunity.
- What earns attention beyond relevance: **original data**, interview access, early or embargoed access, high-resolution images.
- Around half say they are **against AI-generated pitches**. Their top challenge is accuracy and fact-checking under resource constraints.

The synthesis: be relevant, be brief, bring something they cannot get elsewhere, and make their job easier rather than asking them to do yours.

## The announcement decision: exclusive, embargo, or wide

| | What it is | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| **Exclusive** | One outlet gets it, no restriction on when they publish | You want depth and prominence from a specific outlet, and one strong piece beats many shallow ones. The outlet invests because it's differentiated |
| **Embargo** | Multiple outlets get full information in advance, all publish at an agreed time | You want simultaneous breadth with prepared, non-rushed coverage. Requires trust; breaks happen and are rarely punished |
| **Wide release** | Everyone at once, no exclusivity | Genuinely major news that will be covered regardless. Otherwise it maximizes volume of nothing, since no outlet has a reason to go deep |

**On press releases.** The format is fine; the strategy of blasting it is not. Only around 3% of wire-distributed releases result in actual pickup. The structure still holds when you need one: headline carrying a single newsworthy fact, dateline, lede answering most of the five Ws, body, quotes that add perspective rather than restating the headline, boilerplate, contact. One emerging wrinkle worth knowing: releases are now heavily ingested by AI systems, so they increasingly function as machine-readable canonical facts about you as much as journalist bait. That is a reason for accuracy, not for volume.

## Newsjacking

Injecting your perspective into a breaking story so your ideas travel with it. It works when you are genuinely expert in the thing that just happened and you move fast, and the window is short.

It fails catastrophically when the news involves human suffering and your contribution is commercial. The documented failures — brands attaching product promotions to wildfires, co-opting protest imagery — all share one shape: the brand made itself the subject of someone else's tragedy. **Test: if the story involves harm to people, the only acceptable participation is help or silence.**

## Channels beyond the press

- **Thought leadership** has real supporting research on the B2B side: most buyers are not in-market at any given moment, and a majority of decision-makers report willingness to pay a premium for organizations providing genuinely valuable thinking. This is the closest thing to a mechanism for being chosen before you ever pitch.
- **Podcasts** function differently from paid placement: a host choosing you is an implicit endorsement, the episode is evergreen, and the relationship compounds. Genuinely under-researched as a channel — no survey series or academic literature exists — so treat practitioner enthusiasm accordingly.
- **Source platforms.** HARO's history is instructive: founded 2008, absorbed by Cision, rebranded Connectively, moved to paid, shut down entirely at the end of 2024, then relaunched by another owner in its original free form in 2025. Spam and unverifiable credentials killed the first run. Qwoted is the most substantial alternative. These work for commodity quotes and rarely for feature coverage.
- **Awards and speaking.** Be honest about which are pay-to-play: many programs select winners exclusively from paid entrants, which inverts recognition into purchase. They still function as credibility signals to audiences who don't know that. Decide deliberately whether you want to participate in that.
- **Wikipedia.** Paid editing without disclosure has violated the Terms of Use since 2014 and is sanctionable; a paid-editing firm was banned over roughly 250 sockpuppet accounts. Notability guidelines explicitly exclude press releases and routine announcements as qualifying coverage, so a page cannot be manufactured from your own announcements. If you belong there, independent coverage puts you there.

## Measurement

**AVE is discredited and you should refuse to report it.** Advertising Value Equivalency multiplies coverage size by ad rate-card prices, which means a hit piece scores identically to a glowing profile, it prices against rates nobody actually pays, and it measures nothing about whether anyone's belief or behavior changed. It is formally rejected by the industry's own measurement bodies, and multipliers applied on top of it are invented.

The **Barcelona Principles** are the replacement standard, now in their fourth revision. The durable core: set measurable objectives up front; measure **outcomes** (what changed in belief or behavior) rather than **outputs** (what you produced); require both quantitative and qualitative evidence; measure across all channels; report outputs, outcomes *and* organizational impact as distinct tiers; and hold to transparency and replicability, now extended to how AI tools are used in measurement.

In practice, measure: message pull-through (did the coverage carry your actual point, or just your name), audience quality over reach, behavior downstream of coverage, and share of voice against named competitors on the specific issue you're trying to own.

## Watch out

- **The Ries thesis that "PR launches, advertising defends" is unsupported.** It is a widely repeated trade-book claim; no marketing-science study testing it was found in either direction, and the authors ran a consultancy that benefited from it. Directionally reasonable, evidentially empty.
- **Media tiers are not standardized.** Tier 1/2/3 means whatever the shop using it decided. No body defines it. Don't treat someone's tiering as objective.
- **Coverage is not the goal.** A placement nobody in your audience reads, or one that carries your name without your point, is a vanity output. See measurement.
- **Grunig's "two-way symmetrical" ideal is aspirational, not descriptive.** Critics note PR is structurally partisan and the power between an organization and its publics is unequal; Grunig himself later moved toward accepting mixed symmetrical/asymmetrical practice. Use the model to think about whether you are actually willing to be changed by what you hear — which is the honest version of the question.
